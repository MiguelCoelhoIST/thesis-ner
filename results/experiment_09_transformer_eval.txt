Experiment 09 — Transformer role classifier

Goal:
Evaluate a transformer-based contextual classifier for legal role classification of PER entities.

Model:
neuralmind/bert-base-portuguese-cased

Task:
Text classification with entity markers:
[ENTITY] name [/ENTITY] -> legal role label

Labels:
- ARGUIDO
- TESTEMUNHA
- RELATOR
- REU
- UNKNOWN

Training dataset:
data/processed/roles_v3.jsonl
Train examples: 828

Evaluation dataset:
data/eval/roles_eval_realistic.jsonl
Eval examples: 17

Training configuration:
- epochs: 5
- batch size: 8
- learning rate: 2e-5
- max length: 128
- weight decay: 0.01
- best model selected by macro_f1

Runtime:
- train_runtime: 1617.51 seconds
- approximately 26m57s
- training was performed on CPU, no GPU/CUDA detected

Final evaluation:
- accuracy: 0.4706
- macro_precision: 0.4889
- macro_recall: 0.4100
- macro_f1: 0.3952
- eval_loss: 1.2997

Observations:
The training loss decreased strongly, reaching approximately 0.12 overall.
However, evaluation macro F1 remained low at approximately 0.39.

This suggests that the transformer overfitted the synthetic/template-based training data and did not generalize well to the realistic evaluation set.

Interpretation:
The experiment confirms that simply fine-tuning BERTimbau on the current synthetic dataset is not sufficient.
The model appears to memorize training patterns instead of learning robust semantic relations between the marked entity and its legal role.

Conclusion:
Transformer fine-tuning is a valid direction, but requires:
- more realistic training data
- better entity-level examples
- more diverse sentence structures
- possibly real court rulings annotated with LLM assistance
- comparison with encoder embedding approaches suggested by the supervisor

Next steps:
1. Inspect transformer predictions manually.
2. Compare errors against LogisticRegression and rule-based baseline.
3. Try sentence encoder embeddings + classifier.
4. Discuss GPU/IST/INESC machines with supervisor.


Manual prediction analysis:

The transformer correctly handles simple explicit contexts:
- "O arguido [ENTITY] João Ribeiro [/ENTITY]" -> ARGUIDO
- "A testemunha [ENTITY] Carla Sousa [/ENTITY]" -> TESTEMUNHA
- "O relator [ENTITY] António Silva [/ENTITY]" -> RELATOR
- "[ENTITY] Maria Ferreira [/ENTITY] reside em Lisboa." -> UNKNOWN

It also correctly handles an indirect semantic case:
- "O testemunho de [ENTITY] Luís Matos [/ENTITY]" -> TESTEMUNHA

This is an important improvement over the rule-based approach, which struggled with lexical variants such as "testemunho".

However, the model still fails in multi-entity contexts:
- "O arguido João Martins ... testemunha [ENTITY] Ana Silva [/ENTITY]" -> predicted ARGUIDO instead of TESTEMUNHA
- "A testemunha Ana Silva ... arguido [ENTITY] João Martins [/ENTITY]" -> predicted TESTEMUNHA instead of ARGUIDO

It also wrongly assigns the role of another entity in:
- "O réu Pedro Costa foi representado pela mandatária [ENTITY] Sofia Almeida [/ENTITY]" -> predicted REU instead of UNKNOWN

Interpretation:
The model learned useful semantic patterns, but still struggles to bind the legal role to the marked entity in sentences with multiple persons and multiple legal-role terms.

Conclusion:
The transformer shows promising semantic generalization, but the current training dataset does not contain enough difficult multi-entity examples to teach robust entity-role association.
